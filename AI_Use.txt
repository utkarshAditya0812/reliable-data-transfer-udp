AI USE AND TEAM CONTRIBUTION DISCLOSURE
Project: Reliable Data Transfer over UDP

AI tool used: Claude (Anthropic)

AI assistance was used during early development to help generate an initial codebase, suggest project structure, explain implementation approaches, and support troubleshooting. Its output served as a starting point, not a substitute for the team's engineering work or verification.

The students took responsibility for turning that starting point into a working, tested project. The team examined how components fit together, integrated the protocol implementations, ran the application, investigated errors, and checked whether observed behaviour matched reliable data transfer requirements. Work was divided across the implementation: Arijeet Kundu handled checksum and packet handling and Stop-and-Wait; Utkarsh Aditya handled Go-Back-N; Vedant Bhatnagar handled Selective Repeat and retransmission-timeout handling; and Shlok Jaiswal handled the channel emulator, file-integrity utilities, experiments, and result plots.

The team tested all three ARQ protocols—Stop-and-Wait, Go-Back-N, and Selective Repeat—under channel conditions including packet loss and reordering, and resolving issues found during repeated test runs. It reviewed transmission and retransmission behaviour, compared measured results, and used SHA-256 checks to verify that the received file matched the original. These experiments helped the students connect acknowledgements, sequence numbers, timeouts, buffering, and retransmissions with actual program behaviour.

AI assistance was therefore limited to guidance and acceleration during development. The students remained responsible for integration, execution, testing, interpreting results, and understanding the submitted system. This disclosure acknowledges both AI-generated assistance and the team's practical contribution; it does not claim that every line of code was written independently.
